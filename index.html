Six moves, one small lesson

The agent does the patient work of a good learning support aide: read the material, decide the steps, write them kindly, check them, build them, and pace them.

01

Ingest

Paste text, drop a photo of a page, or hand over a PDF or Word file. The original is kept untouched in the lesson folder.

02

Plan

Five to twelve chunks, one idea each, in an order that builds. You see the plan and approve it before anything is written.

03

Write

Ninety words maximum per chunk, sentences under fifteen words, one task each, new words defined as they appear.

04

Gate

Ten automated checks: length, sentence size, reading level, jargon, tasks, quiz coverage. Nothing shipwrecks on a technicality.

05

Build

One self-contained lesson file with navigation, a timer, read-aloud, answer boxes that autosave, and clean printing.

06

Schedule

Optionally, the chunks arrive in chat over days, with recall questions, finishing with a five-question sweep before the test.

The lesson file is yours forever

Everything the agent builds is a single HTML file in your own folder. No account, no server, no expiry date. Open it in any browser, send it to the school, print it, or keep it for next year.

  • ✓Chunk by chunk. Progress, dots, keyboard arrows. One idea on screen at a time.
  • ✓Read aloud. The browser's own voice reads each chunk. Works offline, costs nothing.
  • ✓A gentle timer. Counts up per chunk, remembers where each one got to.
  • ✓Answer boxes that save. Typing is kept in the browser and can be downloaded for the teacher.
  • ✓Print ready. Every chunk, every answer, clean lines on paper.

Knowledge that arrives over days, not all at once

Cramming the night before is the worst way to keep anything. The agent can schedule the chunks into the young person's chat: one a morning, a quick recall question from an earlier chunk, and a mixed sweep the evening before the test.

  • ✓Spacing. Chunks spread across days, front-loaded, quiet hours respected.
  • ✓Retrieval. The recall line before each new chunk is what actually makes it stick.
  • ✓Adaptive. "Don't get it" re-sends that chunk simpler. "Pause" stops everything. "Test moved" re-spaces.
  • ✓Answers kept. Replies are saved into the lesson folder as the young person's working record.

What a delivery looks like

Chunk 3 of 7: Chlorophyll catches light

Quick recall: name the input that enters through the stomata.

Leaves contain a green pigment called chlorophyll. It absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green, which is why leaves look green.

Do: why do leaves look green? Reply with your answer.

No points, no streaks, no guilt. "Done" and "paused" are complete answers.

It scaffolds. It never does the work.

A blank page gets no AI access. The chunking is access: shorter steps, plainer words, a task that can be started. The thinking, the answers and the marks stay the young person's.

Ask it a question and it asks one back first. Ask it to write the essay and it declines, then gets you to the first sentence. Every fact in a chunk traces back to the supplied lesson; if the source is missing something, that becomes a question for the teacher, not an invention.

No diagnosis, no labels, no promises about outcomes. Just patient structure, repeated.
